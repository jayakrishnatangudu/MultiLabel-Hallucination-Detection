# Literature Review

## 1. Thematic Synthesis

Research on LLM hallucination detection (2023–2026) has evolved along five converging trajectories:

1. **Taxonomy Standardization**: Early work divided hallucinations broadly into *intrinsic* vs. *extrinsic* (Ji et al., 2023; Maynez et al., 2020). Huang et al. (2025, `arXiv:2311.05232`) unified LLM-era errors into **Factuality** (*Factual Inconsistency*, *Factual Fabrication*) and **Faithfulness** (*Instruction Inconsistency*, *Context Inconsistency*, *Logical Inconsistency*). HalluLens (Bang et al., ACL 2025) further clarified the boundary between intrinsic grounding errors and extrinsic factuality failures.
2. **Shift from Binary to Fine-Grained & Span-Level Benchmarks**: Coarse binary evaluation (`HaluEval`, Li et al., EMNLP 2023) gave way to segment/span/atomic-level benchmarks such as `FActScore` (Min et al., 2023), `FELM` (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2023), `RAGTruth` (Niu et al., ACL 2024), `FavaBench` (Mishra et al., COLM 2024), `Mu-SHROOM` (Vázquez et al., SemEval-2025 Task 3), `HalluVerse25` / `Halluverse-M³` (Abdaljalil et al., 2025/2026), and `HaluCheck` (Qiu et al., 2026).
3. **Synthetic Hallucination Generation**: Manual annotation at scale is expensive, driving synthetic perturbation frameworks: GPT-4 synthetic training in `MiniCheck` (Tang et al., EMNLP 2024) and `FAVA` (Mishra et al., COLM 2024), EM self-training in `ANAH-v2` (Gu et al., NeurIPS 2024), reasoning-step error injection in `FG-PRM` (Li et al., EMNLP 2025 Findings), and Llama3-8B Termite/prompt perturbations in our base paper (Saxena et al., *LRE* 2025).
4. **Multi-Label & Multi-Category Formulations**: Several works already move beyond single binary labels in specific domains: `SciHal25` (Li et al., ACL SDP 2025) explicitly formulates claim verification against scholarly abstracts as **multi-label classification** (8 fine-grained scientific error types); `FG-PRM` classifies 6 step-level error types in mathematical reasoning; `FAVA` tags 6 span-level error tags. **However**, none of these address *response-level simultaneous co-occurrence across the 5 Huang et al. (2025) categories in high-stakes Medical, Legal, and Financial QA*.
5. **Lightweight Encoder Detectors vs. LLM Judges**: `Luna` (Belyi et al., 2024; DeBERTa-large 440M) and `LettuceDetect` (Kovács & Recski, 2025; ModernBERT up to 8k tokens) demonstrate that compact fine-tuned encoders match or surpass GPT-3.5/GPT-4 prompt judges in RAG grounding at dramatically lower latency and cost, motivating our encoder-based multi-label detector architecture over Saxena et al.'s heavy 7B–13B decoder classifiers.
